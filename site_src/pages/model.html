Keyword search for "train" plus "AI" misses a lot, because companies often say "develop and improve AI" or "machine learning models" without the word "train." So I hand-labelled 133 distinct AI sentences (does this say people's data is used to train, develop or improve AI?) and trained a small model: TF-IDF features over words and word pairs, fed to logistic regression. Company and product names are blacked out before training, so the model has to learn how a training clause is worded, not which company tends to write one.

The model below is the real trained model, exported to the browser: same vocabulary, same weights, same maths.

1

It finds clauses better than keyword search: an F1 score of 0.89 against 0.77.

F1 balances catching every real clause against not flagging the wrong ones; 1 is perfect. I scored it 20 times on sentences it hadn't trained on, and it stayed between 0.84 and 0.91.

With 133 labelled sentences, the interval on the improvement only just clears zero. The model is probably better than keyword search on this kind of data, but 133 labels can't make that airtight.

2

On companies it has never seen, it drops to 0.69, worse than keyword search.

To test this I hid every sentence from a few companies during training and scored the model only on those companies.

This is the result I'd want a reviewer to see first. Ordinary cross-validation lets sentences from the same company sit on both sides of the split, and each company has a house style, so part of the 0.89 is the model recognising house style rather than meaning. For finding clauses at the 25 platforms in this archive, which is all this project uses it for, the ordinary estimate is the relevant one. For a new company's policy, trust the keyword rule, or label a few of its sentences first. That's why, in this project, an unlabelled sentence only counts as a training clause when the model and the keyword rule agree.

Choosing a threshold

The model outputs a probability, and where you draw the line trades precision (of what it flags, how much is right) against recall (of the real clauses, how many it finds). These numbers come from one round of 5-fold cross-validation, so every score is on a sentence the model never trained on. (One round can be a little lucky or unlucky; averaged over twenty rounds with different splits, F1 is 0.887.)
