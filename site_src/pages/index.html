The Fine Print

a data investigation

By August 2026, 13 of the 25 platforms most people use had written one new sentence into their rules: the one that lets them train AI on what you post. The typical one sits halfway down the document.

open the case

The case

Every platform keeps a privacy policy and a terms of service, and they change, quietly and often. Open Terms Archive saves a copy each time the text moves. I pulled every saved version for 25 platforms, 2,283 documents split into 937,131 sentences, and read them the way an investigator reads a paper trail: who changed what, when, in what words, and where they put it.

The findings below are each tested, not eyeballed. Uncertainty comes from a bootstrap that resamples whole platforms, because one company's lawyers write all of its documents; tests are run across platforms for the same reason. The method, the code and the data are public.

1

AI language in force grew 15× in four years.

sentences mentioning AI per 10,000 words in force · first year vs latest year · 95% CI 8.5–30.1× · rose at 12 of the 14 platforms where it moved (sign test p = 0.0065) · 14.1× even without Facebook, the biggest contributor

2

Training clauses hedge 2.05× as much as the rest of the same policy.

hedge words per 100 words, each platform's clauses vs its own other sentences · 95% CI 1.51–2.61× · higher at 9 of 12 platforms (Wilcoxon p = 0.0034)

3

The documents grew from 211,000 to 367,000 words, and got harder to read.

total words in force, every quarter · Kendall τ = 0.94 for length, τ = −0.76 for reading ease (both p < 0.001) · reading all of them once: about 25 hours

4

The typical clause sits halfway down, filed under legal bases.

median training clause sits 49% of the way through its document · most common headings: litigation, legitimate interests, third-party sources
