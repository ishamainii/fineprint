In plain terms

Companies are not copying one standard clause. Each writes its own version, so there is no single phrase you can learn to look for.

Policies are rarely written from scratch. Lawyers reuse language, and platforms with the same owner often share whole sections. I expected the AI clauses to be a few sentences passed between companies. To check, I turned every distinct AI sentence into a TF-IDF vector, a weighted count of its words and word pairs, and measured the cosine similarity of every pair from different platforms. A score of 1 means identical wording. Scores above about 0.5 share long runs of phrasing.

No accomplices. The only exact match is Meta copying Meta.

Facebook and Instagram share a single policy, so their sentences match perfectly. The next closest pair, Pinterest and TikTok, share a phrase, not a sentence. Everyone else wrote their own.

The only close matches come from shared ownership, not from industry boilerplate. This has a downside for readers. Because no one copies a standard clause, there is no common wording to learn once and recognise everywhere. Each platform describes the same practice in its own words and under its own headings, as The Burial shows.
