Policies aren't written from scratch. Lawyers reuse language, and platforms with the same owner often share whole sections. I expected the AI clauses to be the same few sentences passed around. To check, I turned every distinct AI sentence into a TF-IDF vector, a weighted count of its words and word pairs, and measured cosine similarity between every pair of sentences from different platforms. A score of 1 means identical wording, and anything above about 0.5 shares long runs of phrasing.

No accomplices. The only exact match is Meta copying Meta.

Facebook and Instagram share a single policy, so their sentences match perfectly. The next closest pair, Pinterest and TikTok, share a phrase, not a sentence. Everyone else wrote their own.

So the similarity in these documents comes from shared ownership, not industry boilerplate. That cuts both ways. Nobody is copying a standard clause, but there's also no common language a reader could learn once and recognise everywhere. Each platform describes the same practice in its own words, under its own headings (see The Burial).
