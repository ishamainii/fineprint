In plain terms

You won't find the AI clause by skimming. It usually sits in the middle of the document, under a heading about legal grounds or data sources.

People who skim a policy read the start and perhaps the headings. So I measured where AI sentences fall in each current document, from 0 at the first sentence to 1 at the last, and which heading sits above them.

The dig

The box is a document, from its first line to its last. The red layers are where its AI sentences turn up, all current documents stacked together; the dark core is training clauses. Hardly any of it is near the top, where people stop reading.

Exhibit A: excavation

AI language is not pushed to the very end. It is spread through the middle of the documents. The median training clause sits of the way through, about where a skimmer has stopped reading.

Filed under litigation and legitimate interests, not "AI".

The most common heading above an AI sentence is "For litigation". Next is LinkedIn's "When does LinkedIn rely on legitimate interests", then "Third-party public sources". The median training clause sits just under halfway down its document.

The headings above them

The headings are more revealing. Most headings above AI sentences are about legal grounds and data sources, such as "legitimate interests", "third-party public sources" and "partners". Very few mention artificial intelligence. To find the AI language a reader needs to know which legal idea it is filed under.
