Chapter 05The Fine PrintAn investigation by Isha Maini

The Burial

The AI language isn't at the top of these documents, and it's almost never under a heading that says AI. You have to know which legal concept to look under.

If you skim a policy, you read the start and maybe the headings. So I measured where AI sentences fall within each current document, from 0 (the first sentence) to 1 (the last), and which heading they sit under.

The dig

The box is a document, from its first line to its last. The red layers are where its AI sentences turn up, all current documents stacked together; the dark core is training clauses. Hardly any of it is near the top, where people stop reading.

Exhibit A: excavation

There's no strong pattern of burying AI language at the very end. It's spread through the middle of the documents. The median training clause sits of the way through, roughly where a skimmer has stopped and a careful reader is slowing down.

The headings above them

The headings tell a more interesting story. The most common headings above AI sentences are about legal bases and data sources, like "legitimate interests," "third-party public sources" and "partners," rather than anything that says "artificial intelligence." To find the AI language, you need to already know which legal concept it's filed under.
