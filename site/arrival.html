Chapter 1The Fine Print, by Isha Maini

The Confessions

One platform had a training clause at the end of 2022. By August 2026, thirteen of the twenty-five did. Here is each one, in its own words.

In plain terms

More than half of these apps now give themselves permission to train AI on what you post. Most added it between 2023 and 2025. It usually arrived as one new sentence in a routine policy update.

For each platform I found the first saved policy that contains a training clause. That is a sentence saying user information is used to train, develop or improve AI models, or one that gives the legal basis or an opt-out for doing so. Hand-labelled examples and a classifier decide what counts. The Polygraph and Forensics explain both.

The dossiers

Select a card to read the clause as it was first saved.

1

Two waves: mid-2023, as generative AI went mainstream, and mid-2024.

YouTube, Quora, Pinterest and X all added one within eight weeks of each other in the summer of 2023. Instagram, Reddit and LinkedIn followed within five weeks of each other a year later.

How much AI language is in force

For each quarter I took only the documents in force on its last day. I then counted sentences that mention AI per 10,000 words, and how many of those are training clauses. Using documents in force stops a platform that revises every week from being counted fifty times.

2

There's 15 times as much AI language in force as in the first year.

About one sentence on AI per 18,000 words then, one per 1,200 now. Counting individual AI words instead of sentences gives 13 times, and leaving out Facebook, the biggest contributor, gives 14.

The exact figure is uncertain. The starting rate is so close to zero that the multiple changes a lot with the choice of platforms. Resampling the platforms puts it between about 8 and 30. The direction is not in doubt. Of the 14 platforms whose rate changed, 12 went up.

No confession

A missing clause is not proof of innocence. The archive tracks two documents per platform. Several companies describe AI training on separate help pages that it does not track. Meta, for example, announced its training plans in separate notices before the wording reached the Facebook privacy policy.
