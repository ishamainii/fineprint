The Polygraph

The classifier that finds training clauses, running in your browser. Type any sentence and see what it thinks.

Keyword search for "train" plus "AI" misses a lot, because companies often say "develop and improve AI" or "machine learning models" without the word "train." So I hand-labelled 133 distinct AI sentences (does this say people's data is used to train, develop or improve AI?) and trained a small model: TF-IDF features over words and word pairs, fed to logistic regression. Company and product names are replaced with [company] and [product] before training, so the model can't lean on who wrote a sentence. It scores F1 0.89 in cross-validation, against 0.77 for the keyword rule.

Choosing a threshold

The model outputs a probability, and where you draw the line trades precision (of what it flags, how much is right) against recall (of the real clauses, how many it finds). These numbers come from one round of 5-fold cross-validation, so every score is on a sentence the model never trained on. (One round can be a little lucky or unlucky; averaged over ten rounds with different splits, F1 is 0.89.)
